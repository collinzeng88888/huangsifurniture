Office Furniture Quality Control

Quality control workflow for office desks, chairs, workstations, storage cabinets, acoustic panels and office pods.

Quality checks tied to the approved specification

Office furniture inspection is most useful when it refers to the accepted model, drawing, finish sample and packing method. Different products require different checks: a chair has adjustment functions, a workstation has repeated connections, and a reception counter may depend on visible multi-module alignment.

Before Production

Confirm drawings, dimensions, finish references, hardware, product documents and the inspection checklist.

During Production

Review materials, first assemblies, visible finishes and critical functions early enough to correct deviations.

Before Shipment

Check representative finished products, labels, hardware, carton protection and the approved package count.

Information that defines the inspection

Reference documentsApproved drawing, bill of materials, finish sample and order specification
Critical featuresDimensions, stability, moving functions, alignment, locks, cables and accessories
AppearanceVisible surfaces, edge finish, upholstery, color consistency and acceptable variation
PackingHardware count, labels, instructions, protective materials and carton condition

Custom-product approval

Custom dimensions and finishes increase the importance of a signed sample or controlled reference. The approval record should state which aspects are accepted and which remain subject to normal production variation. Screens and online images should not be the only color reference for important projects.

Pre-shipment packing verification

Inspection can confirm that the right product, configuration and finish are in the correctly labeled package. Hardware bags and instructions should correspond to the installation sequence, while visible faces and vulnerable corners need appropriate protection.

Product-specific checkpoints

Chair checks may include adjustments, movement and noise; desks and workstations may require stability, joint and cable-access checks; cabinets need door, drawer, shelf and lock checks. Any load or performance test must follow an agreed method and scope.

Quality control questions

Can one checklist cover every office furniture category?

No. A shared project checklist is useful, but product-specific functions and risks need their own inspection points.

When should inspection criteria be agreed?

Before production release, so quotations, samples and production teams work to the same requirements.

Do test reports apply to every similar-looking model?

Not automatically. Confirm that the report covers the quoted construction, materials and configuration required by the destination market.
